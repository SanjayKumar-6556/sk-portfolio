  Neural emulators make Bayesian inference on the 21-cm signal from the Epoch of Reionization

## My contribution

I am the **second author of nine**. My own work in this line of research is the **21-cm power
spectrum** emulator — the same emulator and inference pipeline I built for my
[MSc thesis](/research/msc-thesis) and presented as **first author** on a
[conference poster](/research/iitm-poster-2024) at IIT Madras in December 2024. The bispectrum
emulator in the paper is collaborators' work; I do not claim it.

The engineering side of the power-spectrum emulator — the Pyro/PyTorch model, the NUTS sampling
over network weights, the likelihood that folds emulator uncertainty into the covariance, and
the MCMC pipeline — is written up as a
[project case study](/projects/bayesian-neural-network).

## Why this matters outside cosmology

The paper is about the early Universe, but the problem it solves is a general one in applied
machine learning: **a fast model standing in for an expensive one is only safe if it can tell
you when it is out of its depth.**

A deterministic network gives one number and no indication of confidence. Downstream, that
number is consumed as though it were exact, and every uncertainty the model itself introduced
disappears from the final answer. Replacing the point estimate with a distribution — and, just
as importantly, making the downstream consumer actually use that distribution — is what turns a
surrogate into something you can put in a decision loop.

The paper's other finding is the one I find most transferable: with plenty of training data both
approaches look fine, and the difference only shows up when training data is scarce. That is
where the deterministic model becomes confidently wrong while the Bayesian one widens its error
bars. Scarce, expensive training data is the normal condition in most real problems, not the
exception.

## Method note

The Bayesian neural network here samples the posterior over network weights with **NUTS/HMC**
rather than fitting a variational approximation. That is a deliberate and more expensive choice:
when the entire claim of the paper is that the emulator's uncertainty is trustworthy enough to
enter a likelihood, approximating that uncertainty would undercut the claim.
