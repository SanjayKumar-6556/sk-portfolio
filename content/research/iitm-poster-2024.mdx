## Why this poster is on the site

It is the clean statement of which part of the [published paper](/research/bnn-cosmology) is
mine. The paper covers both the power spectrum and the bispectrum and lists nine authors; this
poster covers the 21-cm power-spectrum emulator specifically, and I am its first author.

## The argument in four steps

1. **Why emulators at all.** Interpreting a redshifted 21-cm observation means running
   reionization models many times over. Simulations are too expensive to sit inside a sampler;
   a neural emulator is not.
2. **Why ANNs are not enough.** An ANN learns its weights as fixed point values, so it predicts
   a single value with no uncertainty attached — which limits how far its output can be trusted
   in a parameter-inference pipeline.
3. **What a BNN changes.** A BNN learns weights and biases as posterior distributions, so each
   prediction is itself a distribution. That gives uncertainty quantification for free, and the
   uncertainty can then be carried into the inference.
4. **Where the difference actually shows up.** With a large training set both emulators behave
   similarly. As the training set shrinks, the ANN becomes biased toward its training data and
   predicts incorrectly; the BNN stays accurate within its uncertainty range, with the
   uncertainty growing as the data thins.

The conclusion the poster ends on: BNN-based emulators give better parameter constraints than
ANN-based ones precisely because the inference pipeline consumes their uncertainty rather than
ignoring it.
