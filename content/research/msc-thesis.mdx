## What the thesis argues

The Epoch of Reionization is the least understood era in the Universe's history. Next-generation
radio interferometers such as the SKA are expected to observe it through the redshifted 21-cm
line of neutral hydrogen — and the hard part begins once the observation exists, because
interpreting it means comparing against reionization models that are computationally expensive
to run self-consistently.

The thesis takes that problem in three steps:

1. **Make the fast surrogate better where it is actually wrong.** An existing ANN emulator
   (Tiwari et al.) is retrained on a signal library covering a larger portion of the parameter
   space; then the regions of parameter space where the emulator produces erroneous statistics
   are identified, and the training sample density in those regions is increased.
2. **Use it for inference.** The improved emulator is placed inside a Metropolis-Hastings MCMC
   framework to constrain the reionization parameters.
3. **Fix the structural limitation.** An ANN emulator always returns a point estimate with no
   uncertainty attached, so its own prediction error cannot be propagated self-consistently into
   the constrained parameters. A Bayesian neural network emulator is built instead: its weights
   and biases are distributions rather than point values, so its output is a distribution and
   its prediction uncertainty is available to the inference pipeline. Tested across
   progressively smaller training sets, the BNN emulator remains robust where the ANN degrades,
   including under mock SKA-like observation scenarios.

## What it demonstrates for engineering work

Three habits from this thesis carry directly into applied machine learning: diagnosing *where in
the input space* a model fails and fixing the data rather than the architecture; refusing to let
a fast approximate model hide its own error from whatever consumes it; and testing a model under
the condition that actually bites in practice — not enough training data.
